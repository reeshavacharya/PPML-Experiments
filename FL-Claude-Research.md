# Transformer-Based Federated Learning Benchmark: Papers, Configurations, and a Recommended Table for Centralized-vs-Federated Comparison

## TL;DR
- The best-documented, directly reusable transformer FL papers are: **FedNLP (Peng et al., npj Digital Medicine 2024)** for clinical/biomedical NLP (BioBERT/Bio_ClinicalBERT on NCBI-Disease, BC5CDR, JNLPBA, BC2GM, 2018 n2c2; FedAvg + FedProx); **FeSViBS (Almalik et al., MICCAI 2023)** for classification (ViT-B/16 on Fed-ISIC2019 + HAM10000; 6 clients); and **Tölle et al. (npj Digital Medicine 2025)** for segmentation (SWIN-UNETR + ViT under FedAvg, 8 hospitals, 20 rounds, 10 local epochs). For brain-tumor segmentation, the **FeTS2022 challenge line** (Manthe benchmark; FedPIDAvg winner) supplies exact client/round/epoch numbers but uses a fixed 3D U-Net rather than a transformer.
- For NLP, the most popular FL task/dataset is **biomedical Named Entity Recognition (NER)**, with **NCBI-Disease, BC5CDR, JNLPBA, BC2GM and the 2018 n2c2** corpora being the de-facto standard, almost always trained with **FedAvg** (and FedProx for heterogeneity).
- A defensible ~9-row benchmark table spanning image + text is provided below; note that a direct federated SwinUNETR/UNETR paper on **BTCV** and a direct federated **ViT on NIH ChestX-ray14** are gaps in the literature — those rows should be run by the user using MONAI defaults, flagged as "config recommended, not sourced."

## Key Findings

### Segmentation (brain tumor / multi-organ CT)
- **Swin UNETR** and **UNETR** are MONAI-native transformer segmentation models. Swin UNETR (Hatamizadeh et al., arXiv:2201.01266) was validated on BraTS 2021; UNETR and Swin UNETR both have official MONAI BTCV tutorials (13/14-organ CT). These original papers are **centralized**, not federated.
- The **FeTS challenge** (Federated Tumor Segmentation) is the canonical federated brain-tumor benchmark but **fixes a 3D U-Net** (not a transformer) that participants may not modify. FeTS2022 uses the BraTS2021 GBM subset: **1251 training cases + 219 validation cases**, with **Partition 1 = 23 institutions** (natural, highly non-IID) and **Partition 2 = 33 collaborators** (the 5 largest institutions each subdivided into 3 by whole-tumor size).
- **FedPIDAvg** (Mächler et al., arXiv:2304.12117) won FeTS2022; it inherits the 23/33-client partitions and the challenge's fixed 3D U-Net. The number of rounds is bounded by the challenge's **1-week simulated-time budget**, which in practice limits submissions to roughly **≤15 rounds** (the FedPIDAvg paper itself does not state an explicit round count; the ≤15 figure is reported by the related FedPOD paper, arXiv:2512.20610). FedPIDAvg reported test Dice WT 0.768 / ET 0.742 / TC 0.769.
- **Manthe et al. (Medical Image Analysis 2024, arXiv:2410.17265)** is the most rigorous FeTS2022 benchmark: a MONAI **DynUNet 3D U-Net with ~22.5M parameters** (4 downsampling paths, 32→512 channels, instance norm; per the paper, "The network is composed of around 22.5M parameters"), **23 natural institutions**, **~300 FedAvg rounds** (720 for the fixed-local-iteration variant), **1 local epoch per round**, tested under three partitionings — natural institutional split, IID redistribution (to 23/10/5 synthetic clients), and a limited-data subset (18 institutions, 278 patients). FedAvg, FedProx, SCAFFOLD, MOON, FedPer, LG-FedAvg, Ditto, and CFL are all benchmarked; 5-fold federated CV with 80/20 per-institution train/val.
- **Tölle et al. (npj Digital Medicine 2025, arXiv:2407.07557)** is the strongest transformer-in-FL segmentation paper: a real-world **cardiac CT federation of n = 8,104 scans across eight hospitals** ("We conduct the largest federated cardiac CT analysis to date (n = 8,104) in a real-world setting across eight hospitals"), training **UNet, ViT, and SWIN-UNETR** with **FedAvg**, and — per the paper — "Each model was trained for **20 federated rounds** of averaging with **10 local epochs** in each round," plus semi-supervised federated knowledge distillation from a federated UNet teacher into ViT/Swin-UNETR students. SWIN-UNETR only matched the federated UNet when unlabeled data were used via distillation.

### Classification (skin lesion / chest X-ray)
- **FeSViBS (Almalik et al., MICCAI 2023, arXiv:2306.14638)** uses **ViT-B/16 (timm, 12 blocks, embedding dim 768, 12 heads, 16×16 patches)** on **HAM10000 (IID), BloodMNIST (non-IID), and Fed-ISIC2019 (non-IID)**, all with **6 clients** and an 80/20 train/test split. Baselines include centralized, **FedAvg, FedProx (μ=0.006), SCAFFOLD, MOON**, plus split-learning variants (SLViT, FeSTA, SViBS). Fed-ISIC2019 is the FLamby natural 6-center split.
- **FLamby (Ogier du Terrail et al., NeurIPS 2022, pp. 5315–5334, arXiv:2210.04620)** provides the standard **Fed-ISIC2019** benchmark: **23,247 dermoscopy images divided among 6 clients representing different data centers** (per-center train/test counts ≈ 9930/2483, 3163/791, 2961/672, 1807/452, 655/164, 351/88), with a default baseline of **9 federated rounds**, **100 local steps/round**, EfficientNet-B0 (a CNN, not a transformer), and a balanced-accuracy metric. FLamby also includes Fed-IXI (brain MRI). This is the reference suite for open-source cross-silo healthcare FL datasets.
- **HAM10000** (Tschandl, Rosendahl & Kittler, Nature Scientific Data 2018) is **10,015 dermatoscopic images across 7 diagnostic categories**, publicly available through the ISIC archive — the standard IID skin-lesion benchmark.
- For **NIH ChestX-ray14** (Wang et al., 2017: **112,120 frontal-view X-rays from 30,805 unique patients**, each labeled with up to 14 thoracic diseases mined via NLP at >90% accuracy), transformer classifiers (ViT-B/16, Swin) are well documented **centrally** (e.g., SwinCheX, arXiv:2206.04246) but a clean federated ViT-on-ChestX-ray14 paper with full client/round/epoch reporting is a gap; the closest FL chest-X-ray transformer work is ViT+GPT-2 report generation on IU-X-ray (arXiv:2505.21715, FedAvg/Krum/L-FedAvg).

### Clinical/Biomedical NLP
- **FedNLP (Peng, Luo, Zhou, Chen, Xu, Sun, Zhang; npj Digital Medicine 2024; DOI 10.1038/s41746-024-01126-4; code github.com/PL97/FedNLP)** is the definitive federated biomedical NLP study: **2 tasks (NER, RE), 8 corpora, 6 models**. Models: **BERT-base, BlueBERT, BioBERT, Bio_ClinicalBERT, GPT-2, BiLSTM-CRF**. NER datasets: **2018 n2c2, BC2GM, BC5CDR-disease, JNLPBA, NCBI-disease**; RE datasets: 2018 n2c2, euadr, GAD. Aggregation: **FedAvg** (and **FedProx** for heterogeneity). The public code uses **10 data splits (clients)** and **50 total epochs**. Heterogeneity study: **non-IID** simulated by treating **BC2GM and JNLPBA as 2 clients**; **IID** simulated by random splitting of the **2018 n2c2** corpus. Finding: FL (especially pre-trained BERT models) approaches centralized/pooled performance under IID and closes the gap under non-IID with FedProx.
- **NCBI-Disease** (Doğan, Leaman & Lu, J. Biomedical Informatics 2014) is **793 PubMed abstracts with 6,892 disease mentions linked to 790 unique concepts** — "a collection of 793 PubMed abstracts fully annotated at the mention and concept level."
- The most popular FL biomedical NLP task is clearly **biomedical NER**, with NCBI-Disease and BC5CDR the two most reused corpora. NVIDIA FLARE ships an official federated NER example on **NCBI-Disease with BERT/GPT-2**, reinforcing NCBI-Disease as the standard FL NLP benchmark.

### Notes on exact numbers per paper
- **FeTS2022 (Manthe):** 23 institutions; ~300 FedAvg communication rounds (720 for the fixed-local-iteration variant); 1 local epoch/round; MONAI DynUNet ~22.5M params; 3 partitionings (natural, IID to 23/10/5, limited-data 18 institutions/278 patients); 5-fold federated CV; 80/20 per-institution train/val.
- **FeTS challenge / FedPIDAvg:** fixed 3D U-Net (OpenFL + GaNDLF reference implementation); Partition 1 = 23 sites, Partition 2 = 33 sites; rounds bounded by 1-week simulated time (~≤15 effective); epochs/round tunable (default 1); 1251 train + 219 val cases; FedPIDAvg reported Dice WT 0.768 / ET 0.742 / TC 0.769.
- **Tölle et al. 2025:** 8 hospitals (n=8,104); 20 federated rounds; 10 local epochs/round; FedAvg; UNet + ViT + SWIN-UNETR; semi-supervised federated knowledge distillation.
- **FeSViBS:** ViT-B/16; 6 clients on all three datasets; HAM10000 IID (random 80/20), Fed-ISIC2019 + BloodMNIST non-IID; baselines FedAvg, FedProx (μ=0.006), SCAFFOLD, MOON.
- **FLamby Fed-ISIC2019:** 6 natural centers (23,247 images); 9 federated rounds default baseline; 100 local steps/round; EfficientNet-B0 (CNN); balanced-accuracy metric.
- **FedNLP:** 6 models incl. BioBERT/Bio_ClinicalBERT/BlueBERT; NER corpora NCBI-Disease, BC5CDR-disease, BC2GM, JNLPBA, 2018 n2c2; FedAvg + FedProx; 10 splits and 50 epochs in released code; non-IID = BC2GM+JNLPBA, IID = n2c2 random split.

## Recommendations
1. **Anchor each modality on a fully-sourced paper.** Use FedNLP for the two NLP rows (BioBERT/NCBI-Disease and Bio_ClinicalBERT/2018 n2c2), FeSViBS for the two classification rows (ViT-B/16 on Fed-ISIC2019 non-IID and HAM10000 IID), and Tölle et al. for the SwinUNETR/ViT segmentation rows. These give you real, citable client/round/epoch numbers.
2. **For brain-tumor segmentation, adopt the FeTS2022 data + Manthe protocol but swap the fixed 3D U-Net for MONAI SwinUNETR/UNETR.** Keep 23 clients (Partition 1), and either mirror Manthe's ~300 rounds × 1 local epoch, or the FeTS ≤15-round time-bounded regime. Justify the model swap explicitly (transformers capture long-range tumor context; MONAI provides tested, benchmark-validated implementations).
3. **Fill the two literature gaps with MONAI defaults and label them "recommended."** For UNETR on BTCV, start with 4–6 clients, 100–300 rounds, 1–5 local epochs, FedAvg, IID random split (BTCV has only ~50 scans, so IID is more realistic than a synthetic non-IID split). For ViT on NIH ChestX-ray14, use the official patient-wise split, 4–10 clients, FedAvg, 1 local epoch.
4. **Standardize the FL protocol axis.** Use FedAvg as the common baseline across every row (it is the shared denominator in all sourced papers), and add FedProx for the non-IID rows (Fed-ISIC2019, cross-corpus NER, FeTS natural partition) since that is exactly where FedNLP and FeSViBS show it helps. Add FedPIDAvg only for the FeTS row (it is FeTS-specific).
5. **Thresholds that would change the plan.** If a federated ViT-on-ChestX-ray14 paper with full reporting appears, replace row 7's "recommended" config with its numbers; if you need a transformer with a published FeTS2022 result, adopt an OpenFL SwinUNETR run rather than the fixed U-Net once available.

## Caveats
- The FeTS challenge and the strongest FeTS2022 benchmark (Manthe) use a **3D U-Net, not a transformer** — the challenge forbids architecture changes. Any SwinUNETR/UNETR FeTS row is therefore a user-run experiment, not a literature result.
- **BTCV federated** and **NIH ChestX-ray14 federated ViT** rows are recommendations, not sourced results; mark them clearly in any published table.
- FeSViBS is a **federated split-learning** variant; its ViT body sits on the server. If you need pure FedAvg on a whole ViT, use its FedAvg/FedProx baselines (same 6-client Fed-ISIC2019/HAM10000 data) rather than the split-learning numbers.
- FedNLP's "rounds" are expressed as total epochs (50) in released code; the exact round count depends on the local-epoch choice — confirm from the config before quoting a specific number.
- Exact per-round/round-count figures for FeSViBS were not fully captured in the retrieved text (6 clients is confirmed; rounds and local epochs should be verified from the paper's experimental section before publishing).
- The FeTS "≤15 rounds" figure is an *effective* consequence of the 1-week simulated-time limit reported by the FedPOD paper, not a hard integer cap in the challenge rules; and the fixed U-Net's exact filter count (≈30 base filters) comes from the GaNDLF reference implementation, not a verbatim statement in the FeTS Nature Communications paper.
